title: The operators, and how tightly they bind

Part of [[expressions]].  Tightest first; parentheses group and mean nothing
else.

    after an operand    f(x)  a⟦i⟧  d⸨k⸩  p.x  r⌖  x?  a²  ps¨     calls,

    = ≠ < > ≤ ≥         compare: one level, never chained (3014)    [[expressions.comparisons]]

    ⊼ ⊽                 not both, neither: never chained

    ⊕                   exactly one
    ∨ or                or; `or` works out its right side only if needed

So `a & b = c` is `(a & b) = c`, `a < b ∧ c < d` reads as it looks, `2 × a²`
squares first, `a ÷ b ?? c + d` takes each side whole, and `¬ (a < b)` needs
its parentheses.  **A comparison does not chain**: `a < b < c` is refused;
write `a < b ∧ b < c`.

**What is never an operator** a program may define: `∧ ∨ and or`, which
decide whether to work something out; `? ??`, `⌖`, `¨` and `⌜⌝`.  Every other
glyph may be given a meaning for a type of the program's own, a pair of
brackets among them ([[functions.operators]]).  The meanings above cannot be
replaced: `1u8 + 2u8` is three in every program.

**Every operator that works out both of its sides walks arrays**, element by
element ([[types.arrays.walking]]).  `-` is only subtraction: a negative
number is written `⁻3i8`, and there is no unary minus.
