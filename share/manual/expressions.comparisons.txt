title: Comparisons and logic

Part of [[expressions]].

**Six comparisons**, each answering a `bool`: `=` `≠` `<` `>` `≤` `≥`
(`<=` and `>=` are accepted for the last two).  `=` compares and never
assigns -- assigning is `←` -- so a comparison standing as a statement is
refused (5005).  Both sides have one type.  They bind looser than everything
that computes a number and do not chain (3014).

    numbers, char, str      =  ≠  and the order: by value, by code point
    bool, an enumeration    =  ≠  only: no order is promised
    a set, a dictionary     =  ≠  whether they hold the same entries
    a record, a sum         none yet

Text is ordered character by character, a prefix first: `"ab" < "abc"`, and
`"Z" < "a"` -- an order of code points, not a collation.  `=` and `≠` on
floating-point numbers are warned about (4217).

**Six tolerant comparisons** ask of floating-point numbers only (4218), against
`⎕tolerance` -- a `mut f64`, 10⁻¹³ until the program sets it:

    a ≅ b   |a - b| ≤ t        a ≇ b   |a - b| > t
    a ⪅ b   a - b ≤ t          a ⪆ b   b - a ≤ t
    a ⪉ b   b - a > t          a ⪊ b   a - b > t

**The logical operators** take truth values and nothing else:

    a ∧ b   both          a ∨ b   at least one      a ⊕ b   exactly one
    a ⊼ b   not both      a ⊽ b   neither           ¬a      not
    a and b, a or b       the same as ∧ and ∨, with the right side worked out
                          only where the left does not settle it

They bind looser than the comparisons, so `a < b ∧ c < d` needs no
parentheses; `∧` binds tighter than `⊕`, which binds tighter than `∨`, and
`⊼` and `⊽` do not chain.  `¬` binds tightly: `¬ (a < b)` needs its
parentheses.  Only `and` and `or` skip their right side, and they do not walk
arrays; the glyphs do.

**`b ∣ a`** -- does `b` divide `a` -- binds as a comparison too
([[expressions.arithmetic]]).

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    fn inside(x: f64, low: f64, high: f64) → bool:
        x ⪆ low ∧ x ⪅ high

    @[startup]
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        let near: bool = inside((1f64 ÷ 3f64 ?? 0f64) × 3f64, 1f64, 2f64)
        let words: bool = "apple" < "apples" ∧ "Zebra" < "apple"
        let one: bool = (3u8 > 2u8) ⊕ (2u8 > 3u8)
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{} {} {}", near, words, one⌝
        0u6

Output:

    true true true
