title: Arithmetic

Part of [[expressions]].

    a + b   a - b   a × b      checked: an answer that does not fit stops
                               the program (status 80)
    a ÷ b   a % b              answer a result: T?
    a ⊞ b   a ⊟ b   a ⊠ b      saturating: the nearest value the type holds
    ⎕wrap(EXPR)                every operator inside keeps the low bits
    a ⁿ b   a²   a⁻²           powers
    b ∣ a   b ∤ a   ∣n   ∤n    divides, does not divide, even, odd

**Checked by default.**  `200u8 + 100u8` stops the program with a message
saying where and how it got there; where both sides are written down the
compiler refuses it (4214).  A check the program can never fail is not made.

**Division truncates toward nought**, and `%` keeps the sign of what was
divided: `⁻7i8 ÷ 2i8` is `⁻3i8`, `⁻7i8 % 2i8` is `⁻1i8`.  Both answer a
result, which has no answer for a divisor of nought or for the most negative
number divided by `⁻1` -- so `(a ÷ b) ?? 0u8`, or `(a ÷ b)?`.

**Saturating arithmetic** answers the end of the range instead of going past
it: `200u8 ⊞ 100u8` is 255 and `5u8 ⊟ 9u8` is nought.  **`⎕wrap(EXPR)`**
makes `+ - ×` keep the low bits and a shift take its distance modulo the
width, for the operators written inside it and no further -- a function called
there checks as it always does.  A saturating operator inside a wrap is
refused, the two contradicting each other.

**A power**: `a ⁿ b` answers `T?`, the exponent being a count that may turn
out negative, and a negative power being one divided by the positive one.
`a²` and `a¹⁴` are written raised, never negative, and answer `T`; `a⁻²`
answers `T?`.  What is raised is a number, integer or floating-point
(4509); what it is raised by is a whole number (4510), so `1.5f64³` is
3.375.  It binds tighter than `×` and to the right; anything to the nought
is one.

**`b ∣ a` asks whether `b` divides `a`**, and binds as a comparison.  With
the divisor written down it answers `bool`; worked out, `bool?u8`, since it
may be nought: `(b ∣ a) ?? false`.  A nought written there is refused (4520).
`∣n` and `∤n` ask whether `n` is even and odd.

These are defined on whole numbers, and `+ - × ÷` on floating-point ones
([[types.floats]]); bits are [[expressions.bits]].

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    fn hash(x: u32) → u32:
        ⎕wrap(x × 2654435761u32 + 12345u32)

    @[startup]
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        let full: u8 = 250u8 ⊞ 10u8
        let none: u8 = 5u8 ⊟ 9u8
        let q: i8 = (⁻7i8 ÷ 2i8) ?? 0i8
        let r: i8 = (⁻7i8 % 2i8) ?? 0i8
        let cube: u16 = 7u16³
        let even: bool = ∣cube
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{} {} {} {} {} {} {}", full, none, q, r, cube, even, hash(1u32)⌝
        0u6

Output:

    255 0 ⁻3 ⁻1 343 false 2654448106
