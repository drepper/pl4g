title: Truth values

Part of [[types]].

`bool` has two values, `true` and `false`, and nothing else is one: a number
is no condition, so `if n:` is refused for a number `n` (4427) and
`n ≠ 0u8` asks what was meant.  Nothing converts between `bool` and a number
in either direction.  What a `bool` occupies is the compiler's business.

**The logical operators** take truth values and nothing else:
`∧` and, `∨` or, `⊕` exactly one, `⊼` not both, `⊽` neither, `¬` not; and the
words **`and` and `or`**, which do not work out their right side where the left
settles the answer.  `&` and `|` are no logical operators, a `bool` having no
bits a program may see ([[expressions.comparisons]]).

**Comparing** two truth values asks only `=` and `≠`: neither comes before
the other, so `<` is refused.

A comparison answers a `bool`, and so does a set asked about a key, `s⸨k⸩`,
and a test function ([[running.testing]]).

    let ready: bool = count > 3u8 ∧ ¬done
    if ready and expensive(): …       ※ expensive() runs only where ready
