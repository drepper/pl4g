title: Arenas and the heap

Part of [[memory]].

**An arena is a pool**: room is taken from it by adding to a pointer, nothing
is given back one object at a time, and all of it is given back at once.  It is
a variable of type `arena`, always started `⎕arena` (4444), inside a function
or at the top level:

    let scratch: mut arena = ⎕arena

**It is given back when the block it was defined in is left** -- off its end,
by `return`, `break`, `continue` or `?` -- after what the block put off and
after what the block comes to is worked out.  Nothing else gives one back, and
nothing writes `⎕empty` (4932): an arena wanted afresh each turn is defined
inside the loop.

**`EXPR in NAME` says which arena an expression takes its room from**:

    let both: str = first ⧺ second in scratch
    let said: str = ⍕v in scratch
    let made: str = std.text(v, .base ← 16u8) in scratch
    let d: ⸨str: u8⸩ = ⸨"a": 1u8⸩ in scratch

What may carry `in` is what takes room -- a join, a collection written out, an
operator or call that takes an arena, a walk's frame -- and anything else is
refused (4568).  **A call that leaves out an arena parameter is handed the
arena in force** -- the one its `in` names, or `⎕heap` -- so `std.text(v)`
inside `… in scratch` is made in `scratch`.

**`⎕heap`** is the allocator everything comes from that names no other, and
gives back one object at a time.  **A variable whose list, text or record of
them is its own gives it back where its scope ends**, by every way out, unless
it handed it on first -- answered it, gave it to another name or a field, a
table or a parameter the callee takes as its own, or a lambda brought it in --
and one given something new gives back what it held.  What another may still
hold is left where it is.  A function answering text it made answers it in the
heap unless its signature names an arena ([[memory.answers]]).

**A value made in an arena does not outlive it**: a name bound further out may
not be given one (4614), and what a function answers out of an arena of its
own is copied into the caller's allocator first.  The compiler follows every
way through a body to say so; the report log shows which allocator each value
got (`allocator`) and every copy (`copy-into-allocator`).

**`std.print` and `std.println` make a pool of their own** at each invocation,
so a printed line takes room only while it is written.

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    fn label(a: &mut arena, n: u16) → str in a:
        "#" ⧺ ⍕n in a

    @[startup]
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        let kept: mut str = ""
        foreach i := 1u16…4u16:
            let scratch: mut arena = ⎕arena
            let tmp: str = label(&mut scratch, i) ⧺ "!" in scratch
            kept ← kept ⧺ tmp              ※ copied into ⎕heap: tmp goes with scratch
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{}", kept⌝
        0u6

Output:

    #1!#2!#3!
