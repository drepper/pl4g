title: defer

Part of [[statements]].

**`defer STATEMENT`** puts a statement off until **the block it is written in
is left**, whichever way: off its end, by `return`, by a `break` or `continue`
leaving it, by a `?` handing a failure on.  The last put off runs first, and
only what was reached runs.  What the block comes to is worked out **before**
anything put off runs, so a deferred assignment does not change what was
answered.

    let from: mut std.io.Reader = r
    defer from.close()                 ※ closed however the block is left

**What may be put off is something to do**: a call or an assignment.  Not a
`return`, `break`, `continue` or `?`, not a `let`, and not another `defer`
(4612).  It means what it would have meant where it was written.

**A loop's body is a block**, so a `defer` in it runs at the end of every
turn.  An arena is given back the same way when the block that made it is
left, after what the block put off ([[memory.arenas]]); a value that has to
be closed is closed by a `defer` ([[memory.unique]]).  A generator left part
way through runs what it put off when the loop over it is left
([[functions.generators]]).

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    @[impure]
    fn note(trail: &mut u64, n: u64):
        trail⌖ ← trail⌖ × 10u64 + n

    @[impure]
    fn steps(trail: &mut u64, early: bool) → u8:
        defer note(trail, 1u64)
        if early:
            return 7u8
        defer note(trail, 2u64)
        note(trail, 3u64)
        8u8

    @[startup, impure]
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        let trail: mut u64 = 0u64
        let a: u8 = steps(&mut trail, false)
        let b: u8 = steps(&mut trail, true)
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{} {} {}", a, b, trail⌝
        0u6

Output:

    8 7 3211
