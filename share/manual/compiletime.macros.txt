title: Macros

Part of [[compiletime]].

**A macro is invoked with lifting marks around its arguments**, `twice⌜3u8⌝`,
and is handed what is written there as written -- a piece of the program, not
what it evaluates to -- and replaced by what it writes.  A macro and a function
are not in one namespace.  An invocation stays on one line.

**A macro of rules** lists what the arguments look like and what the
invocation becomes, tried in order, the first that matches deciding (7012
where none does).  In a pattern **`$a` is a hole** matching anything; a hole
written twice matches only where the two are written alike; anything else
matches itself.  A template is one expression (7019), or statements indented
under the opening mark:

    macro pick:
        ⌜$x, $x⌝ → ⌜1u8⌝             ※ written alike
        ⌜$x, $y⌝ → ⌜0u8⌝             ※ anything else

    macro swap:
        ⌜$a, $b⌝ → ⌜
            let t: u8 = $a
            $a ← $b
            $b ← t
        ⌝

**A macro writing statements is invoked on a line of its own**, its statements
going into the run around it; where a value stands instead they are a scope of
their own and the last is an expression (7014), what it comes to.

**Hygiene**: a name a macro binds is renamed to something no source can spell,
so `swap`'s `t` cannot meet the caller's `t`; a name it reads means what it
means where the macro was written, through that module.  The one exception is
`⎕caller_name`, by which `std.format` reads `{name}` at its caller.

**Expansion comes after parsing and before any check**: what the checker sees
has no macro left in it, so every rule of the language is asked of what the
macro wrote, and every message points where the offending text was written --
in the invocation or in the macro.  A macro writing another is expanded in
turn, at most 64 times over (7015).

**A macro may be exported**, and is invoked through its module:
`std.println⌜…⌝` ([[std.println]]).  Macros written as functions over the
program's text, and `comptime fn`, are [[compiletime.macros.functions]].

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    macro twice:
        ⌜$x⌝ → ⌜$x + $x⌝

    macro swap:
        ⌜$a, $b⌝ → ⌜
            let t: u8 = $a
            $a ← $b
            $b ← t
        ⌝

    @[startup]
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        let t: mut u8 = 1u8
        let u: mut u8 = 2u8
        swap⌜t, u⌝
        let four: u8 = twice⌜u⌝
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{} {} {}", t, u, four⌝
        0u6

Output:

    2 1 2
