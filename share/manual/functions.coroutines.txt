title: Coroutines and tasks

Part of [[functions]].

**A generator written with `⇇ U` takes a `U` back each time it is resumed**,
and its `yield` answers it.  It is driven by hand:

    fn running_total(start: u64) ⇉ u64 ⇇ u64:
        let total: mut u64 = start
        while true:
            let more := yield total
            total ← total + more

**`⎕start(w)`** makes a walk under way out of a coroutine's call -- or out of
anything a loop walks -- and **`⎕resume(c, u)`** hands `u` in and takes the
next turn, as a result that fails once the walk has nothing more; one that
takes nothing back is resumed `⎕resume(c)` (4678).  The first value handed in
is not seen, the body having reached no `yield` yet.  A coroutine that takes
values back is never walked by a loop (4677).  **`⌖⇉T ⇇ U`** is the type of a
walk under way, a handle: it may be a parameter, and nothing that outlives the
call that started it (4670) -- unless it was started in the heap,
`⎕start(g(…) in ⎕heap)`, of type `⌖⇉T ⇇ U in ⎕heap`, which goes anywhere; what
such a walk is handed has to last as long (4680).

**`⎕await(v)` is `yield v`**, under the name of what a task does with it.

**A task is a coroutine `⇉ std.io.Pending ⇇ std.io.Answer`**: it hands out each
request it starts and is handed back what the kernel said.  `std.spawn(&mut
init.fs, ⎕start(t(…) in ⎕heap))` hands one over and `std.run(&mut init.fs)`
drives every task until each has ended, answering how many did.  The tasks
share one thread, in rounds: each goes on as soon as what it waits for has
come, and a round in which none can waits for the kernel:

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    @[io]
    fn say(what: u8⟦⟧) ⇉ std.io.Pending ⇇ std.io.Answer:
        match std.fs.cwd().open("/dev/null", ⌜std.io.Writer⌝):
            std.io.Writer(to):
                let out: mut std.io.Writer = to
                defer out.close()
                match std.write(&mut out, what):
                    std.io.Pending(p):
                        _ ← std.done(⎕await(p))
                    ⊥:
                        return
            ⊥:
                return

    @[startup, impure]                    ※ spawn keeps the task in init.fs
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        std.spawn(&mut init.fs, ⎕start(say(⎕bytes("one\n")) in ⎕heap))
        std.spawn(&mut init.fs, ⎕start(say(⎕bytes("two\n")) in ⎕heap))
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{} tasks ran", std.run(&mut init.fs)⌝
        0u6

Output:

    2 tasks ran

A task waits in a helper by handing it the walk, `yield ⇉ helper(…)`, the
helper being a generator of the task's own type.  It reads with
`std.read_start` and `std.received` ([[std]]).

A coroutine driven by hand:

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    fn running_total(start: u64) ⇉ u64 ⇇ u64:
        let total: mut u64 = start
        while true:
            let more := yield total
            total ← total + more

    @[startup]
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        let c := ⎕start(running_total(10u64))
        let a := ⎕resume(c, 0u64) ?? 0u64
        let b := ⎕resume(c, 5u64) ?? 0u64
        let d := ⎕resume(c, 7u64) ?? 0u64
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{} {} {}", a, b, d⌝
        0u6

Output:

    10 15 22
