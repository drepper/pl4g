title: Counting, shaping and joining arrays

Part of [[types.arrays]].

**`#a`** is how many along the outermost dimension, a `u64 ¤size`: rows of a
table, not elements.  The same `#` counts a list, a tuple, a table's entries
and a string's characters.

**`⍴` is shape.**  Before one array it answers the shape -- a `u64` for one
dimension, a tuple of them for more.  Between a shape and values it makes an
array of that shape, filled in order and **going round again** where the
values run out; more values than fit are refused (4498).  The shape has to be
known while compiling (4496), and none of it nought (4497).

    ⍴m                         ※ 〈2, 3〉 of a u8⟦2,3⟧
    3 ⍴ 7u8                    ※ u8⟦3⟧: 7 7 7
    6 ⍴ ⟦0u8, 1u8⟧             ※ 0 1 0 1 0 1
    〈2, 3〉 ⍴ v                 ※ a table out of v's elements, round again
    let buf: mut u8⟦1024⟧ = 1024 ⍴ 0u8

**`a ⧺ b` joins along the first dimension**, every other staying as it was
(4492): two tables of three columns make one of three columns.  Both sides
hold one type (4491) and state their shape (4490).  `++` is its substitute.
`⧺` joins strings and lists as well.

**`⌈` and `⌊`** between two values answer the larger and the smaller;
before one array, the largest and the smallest element -- of a table, a row
whose *j*-th is the largest *j*-th.  Of nothing it stops the program (82).
`⌈⌜T⌝` and `⌊⌜T⌝` are a type's extremes.

**`⁂a` spreads an array whose type says its length** into a call's arguments
or a tuple's members, an element each -- a table a row each
([[expressions.calls]]).

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    fn sum3(a: u8, b: u8, c: u8) → u8:
        a + b + c

    @[startup]
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        let v: u8⟦3⟧ = ⟦4u8, 9u8, 2u8⟧ in ⎕static
        let w: u8⟦2⟧ = ⟦7u8, 1u8⟧ in ⎕static
        let both: u8⟦5⟧ = v ⧺ w
        let pattern: u8⟦6⟧ = 6 ⍴ ⟦0u8, 1u8⟧
        let n: u64 = ⎕drop(#both)
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{} {} {} {}", n, ⌈both, sum3(⁂v), pattern⟦5⟧⌝
        0u6

Output:

    5 9 15 1
