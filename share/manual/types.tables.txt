title: Sets and dictionaries

Part of [[types]].

**`⸨T⸩` is a set and `⸨K: V⸩` a dictionary**, written as their values are:
`⸨1u8, 2u8⸩`, `⸨"a": 1u8, "b": 2u8⸩`.  A colon after the first entry makes a
dictionary.  An empty `⸨⸩` is whichever the type wanted says (4432).  Both
hash their keys, with a seed of each table's own, so no input can be chosen to
make lookups slow; `PL4G_HASH_SEED=n` in the environment repeats a run.

**A key** is a type `=` answers exactly on: the whole numbers, `bool`,
`char`, an enumeration and `str` (4429) -- no floating-point number, record or
sum.  A value may be anything but `void` or another table (4430, 4445).

    s⸨k⸩                        ※ bool: does the set hold k
    d⸨k⸩                        ※ V?: the value, or a result holding none
    d⸨k⸩ ?? 0u8                 ※ or this instead
    d⸨k⸩ ← v                    ※ put or replace a value
    †d⸨k⸩                       ※ take the key out, answering what d⸨k⸩ would

**Putting anything in needs `mut` in the type**, `let d: mut ⸨str: u8⸩`
(4598): a table is a handle, so what may be done to it is part of its type,
and a writable one stands where a read-only one is wanted.  A set takes a key
by a join, `s ← s | ⸨k⸩`.

**A table is walked in the order its keys were first put in**: `foreach k :=
s`, and `foreach k, v := d` for a key and its value.  `#t` is how many entries
it holds.  **`|`, `&`, `^` and `-` join two sets** -- either, both, one and
not the other, the first without the second -- with Python's meanings and
order.  `=` and `≠` ask whether two tables hold the same; there is no order.

A table is made in the arena `in` names or in `⎕heap`; one written down that
never changes says `in ⎕static` and is laid out in the image, and one written
empty at the top level costs nothing until something is put in it.
`⎕environ` is the environment, a `⸨str: str⸩` ([[builtins]]).

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    fn counted(words: str⟦⟧) → ⸨str: u8⸩:
        let seen: mut ⸨str: u8⸩ = ⸨⸩
        foreach w := words:
            seen⸨w⸩ ← (seen⸨w⸩ ?? 0u8) + 1u8
        seen

    @[startup]
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        let words: str⟦5⟧ = ⟦"to", "be", "or", "not", "to"⟧ in ⎕static
        let d: ⸨str: u8⸩ = counted(words)
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{} {}", d, d⸨"to"⸩ ?? 0u8⌝
        0u6

Output:

    ⸨to: 2, be: 1, or: 1, not: 1⸩ 2
