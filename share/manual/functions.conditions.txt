title: Conditions

Part of [[functions]].

**`pre(EXPR)` and `post(EXPR)` in a signature say what must hold** of a call's
values, one expression per clause and as many clauses as there are things to
say, between the header and the body:

    fn twice(a: u8) → u8 pre(a > 0u8) post(⎕answer > a):
        a ⊞ a

**A `pre` is checked at the top of the body and a `post` before every
return**, in the callee: a `pre` that fails exits with status 89, the caller's
fault, and a `post` with 90, the callee's.  `⎕answer` is what the function
answers, and only a `post` names it (4908); `⎕entry(NAME)` is what a parameter
held when the function was entered, for one the body changed -- a parameter's
name and nothing else (4920, 4921), only in a `post` (4919).

**A condition is a `bool`** (4906) and pure, whatever its function may do.  One
that comes to a constant falsehood is refused (4918); one that comes to a
constant truth costs nothing.  A condition over the types a call gives --
`pre(⌜T'⌝ = ⌜u8⌝ ∨ ⌜T'⌝ = ⌜u16⌝)` -- is settled at the call.

**Clauses over types are requirements**, which are another thing: what a
generic function needs of its types ([[functions.requirements]]).  A clause
mixing values and types is refused (4905).

**What a build does with conditions** is `--conditions=…`:

    check     evaluate, stop the program where one does not hold (the default)
    ignore    evaluate nothing, emit nothing
    observe   evaluate, report where one does not hold, and go on

Every build asks the same questions of the clauses, so nothing compiles only
because the checks are off; the report log lists the conditions a build left
out (`condition-dropped`) and those the compiler settled (`condition-holds`).
A generic function's conditions are checked in each body it becomes.

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    fn mean(xs: u16⟦⟧) → u16 pre(#xs > 0) post(⎕answer ≤ ⌈xs):
        let sum: mut u32 = 0u32
        foreach x := xs:
            sum ← sum + ⎕widen(x, ⌜u32⌝)
        let n: u32 = ⎕narrow(⎕drop(#xs), ⌜u32⌝) ?? 1u32
        ⎕narrow((sum ÷ n) ?? 0u32, ⌜u16⌝) ?? 0u16

    @[startup]
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        let v: u16⟦4⟧ = ⟦3u16, 5u16, 9u16, 7u16⟧ in ⎕static
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{}", mean(v)⌝
        0u6

Output:

    6
