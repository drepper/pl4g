title: Borrowing

Part of [[memory]].

**A `&mut` is the only reference to its place while it lives; a `&` may share
with other `&`s** (4563).  Whoever holds a `&mut` knows nothing else changes
the place, and whoever holds a `&` that nothing does.  **The name counts as a
way to the place**: while a `&mut` is out the name is not read (4565), and
while any reference is out it is not written (4566).

**A borrow lasts until the last use of what keeps it**, on each way through --
after the statement that uses it last, at the start of an arm that never uses
it, after a loop that does.  One nothing keeps, handed straight to a call, ends
with the call.  So a place may be lent again on the next line:

    let a: &mut u8 = &mut n
    a⌖ ← 2u8                       ※ a's last use: n is free from here
    let b: &mut u8 = &mut n        ※ no second one out
    bump(&mut p) + bump(&mut p)    ※ lent twice, one after the other

**Two fields are two places**, and so are two elements whose indices are
written down: `&mut v⟦0⟧` and `&mut v⟦2⟧` may be out at once, and
`copy(&mut init.io.input, &mut init.io.output)` lends two fields.  An element
whose index is worked out while running is the whole array.  A field and the
record holding it are one place.

**Copying a `&mut` is a reborrow**: `let other: &mut u8 = r` borrows from `r`
as `&mut x` borrows from `x`, and `both(r, r)` is refused.  A `&` copied from
it shares, so `r` may still be read.  An arena is not counted.

**Borrowing reaches beyond references**: a view of a list borrows the list, an
iterator borrows what it walks (4667), and a loop borrows what it walks
(4566).  Variables at the top level are not tracked, every function reaching
them.

**The report log** says where each borrow ended and why (`borrow-ended`),
which is the range the compiler assumed.  This is Rust's rule with its
non-lexical lifetimes; nothing of it costs anything while the program runs.

Program:

    let std := ⎕import("std")

    @[impure]
    fn swap(a: &mut u8, b: &mut u8):
        let t: u8 = a⌖
        a⌖ ← b⌖
        b⌖ ← t

    @[startup, impure]
    fn main(init: std.Init) → u6:
        let v: mut u8⟦3⟧ = ⟦1u8, 2u8, 3u8⟧
        swap(&mut v⟦0⟧, &mut v⟦2⟧)         ※ two elements, two places
        let n: mut u8 = 5u8
        let r: &mut u8 = &mut n
        r⌖ ← r⌖ + 1u8                      ※ r's last use
        n ← n × 2u8                        ※ so n may be written again
        _ ← std.println⌜&mut init.io.output, "{} {} {}", v⟦0⟧, v⟦2⟧, n⌝
        0u6

Output:

    3 1 12
